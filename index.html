Evidence grades

Strong
Meta-analysis or several RCTs pointing the same way.
Moderate
At least one randomised or controlled trial, or a meta-analysis of mixed-quality studies.
Preliminary
Small, open-label, case-control or single-session studies.
Contested
Blinded, sham-controlled or pooled data that fail to support the effect, or a marker that does not hold up diagnostically.

Confidence rating

Each group of studies gets a rating from High to Very low, following a simplified GRADE approach:

  • Start from the strongest supporting design: meta-analysis or randomised trial = High, controlled study or cohort = Moderate, case-control or review = Low, uncontrolled = Very low.
  • Rate down one level if any study points the other way or fails to replicate, and one level if the support is a single primary study (reviews and meta-analyses already pool several).
  • For treatments, if no blinded or sham-controlled study supports the effect, the rating is capped at Low.
  • Each card shows design (1–5 bars), type of control, and sample size where it has been recorded.

Reading the studies critically

    About this map

    EEG markers are from resting-state recordings only (eyes open or closed); task, evoked-response and sleep studies are left out. Group-level EEG differences are not diagnostic tests for individuals, and the same pattern turns up in several conditions. Summaries were compiled from the published literature and are a starting point, not a clinical guide. Check every claim against the original paper before using it with clients. Scalp electrodes pick up activity spread from nearby cortex (volume conduction), and readings also depend on the reference and montage, so findings at neighbouring electrodes are shown too (dashed). Old 10-20 names are used: T3/T4/T5/T6 are T7/T8/P7/P8 in the 10-10 system.
